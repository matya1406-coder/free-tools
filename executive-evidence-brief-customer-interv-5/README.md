## One-page executive summary

**Decision:** Test a narrow service that summarizes existing written customer interview notes. These excerpts support exploring that service, but do not establish demand for this exact offer or its price.

**What the evidence says:** One commenter explicitly says they would pay for executive summaries and praises a prior summary [S1:L1]. Separately, a GitHub excerpt names a desired interview summary covering pains, willingness to pay, and scope changes [S2:L1]. These signals overlap around summarization, but describe different contexts.

**Problem and requested output:** The clearest requested artifact is a synthesis of pains, payment statements, and scope changes [S2:L1]. The excerpt does not itself say that reading the notes takes too long, that the author needs an external supplier, or that the notes are accessible. Those remain assumptions about the proposed service.

**Willingness to pay:** There is one explicit, unpriced statement of willingness to pay for executive summaries in general [S1:L1]. There is no evidence of a purchase, budget, acceptable price, or willingness to pay for this particular interview-report service. The phrase “willingness to pay” in S2 is a requested research topic, not the author's own offer to pay.

**Scope implication:** A useful pilot should produce a short summary, a source-linked pain table, a payment-statement table, and unresolved scope questions. Include a separate scope-implications column because S2 explicitly requests scope changes. Require existing written notes; do not infer authorization or readiness to conduct interviews from the issue text.

**Recommended next decision:** Offer a free, bounded pilot and check whether either person has relevant anonymized notes and wants this output. Do not treat these two records as two committed customers. Do not set a validated paid price from these excerpts.
